# Prompt locks: lines that go into EVERY image prompt and EVERY video prompt (all rows)

1. PROPORTION LOCK: "Keep her face, head size, facial proportions and body proportions exactly the same as in the master frame: identical face shape, eye spacing, nose, mouth size, jaw and head-to-shoulder ratio, the same distance from the camera, and every product at its true relative size. Do not stretch, widen, shrink or redraw her face between frames."
2. NO DUPLICATES: "Every product appears exactly once in the picture. A product in her hand is NOT also on the table or anywhere else. The count of products visible equals the row's count exactly. Two similar-looking products (for example two sizes of the same perfume) must stay clearly different in size and label."
3. NATURAL GESTURE: "Every action is an ordinary everyday gesture with a natural arm and body position. Never an arm raised above the head, never spraying or applying on the head, never an awkward or contorted pose."

Where they live: `omni.py` adds locks 1-3 to every clip prompt automatically; image prompts must include them (copy the three lines). `check_actions.py` fails any action file or storyboard that contains an unnatural-gesture phrase or lacks the locks.
4. ZOOM LIMIT: "During an application the camera push-in is gentle and small: the framing tightens by no more than about 20 percent over the whole clip (for example from a medium-close shot to a close-up of the face), at constant speed. No extreme close-up, no macro that fills the frame with the lips, eye or skin, and never a zoom that makes her face or the product leave the frame; her face and the area being worked on both stay in view."

4b. ZOOM MINIMUM (user 2026-11-04, tightens lock 4): "Keep camera movement to a minimum: nearly locked off, at most a barely noticeable push-in of about 10 percent over the whole clip at constant speed; no zoom in or out, no extreme close-up or macro."


## 5. FRAMING LOCK (user 2026-11-05, ID 45)
All first frames of a run keep the hook frame's camera position, shot size, room and her seated position, so cuts between clips do not jump in framing; the face stays the same size and place in every frame. Products she is not holding stay on the table in the same places. A product she holds is REMOVED from the table: generating with the hook frame as the only table reference makes the model keep the old copy (duplicate). So for every frame the prompt names the emptied spot, and the check below is mandatory before any clip: count each product in the frame (held + table) and reject the frame if any product appears twice. Never launch clips from a frame that has not passed this count.

## 6. ROOM LOCK (user 2026-11-05)
One room setting is selected per run and locked via a single empty-room plate used as the base of every frame; see PIPELINE_CONCEPT_LAB.md 'Room setting is always selected'.

## 7. REALISM LOCK (user 2026-11-05, ID 16: kajals floated, hands looked pasted on a different background)
Hands-only / POV frames are shot as a flat-lay DIRECTLY FROM ABOVE on a real surface (linen, tray, wood): every product lies flat with a soft contact shadow or is gripped in a natural pinch grip; nothing stands unsupported, leans, balances or floats (standing blister cards and balanced pencils always look fake). Hands have forearms and sleeves entering from the frame edge, one natural skin tone, same light direction and colour temperature as the surface. Do not use a tall sharp backdrop (mirror, flowers) behind hands-only products. The sentence is added to every clip prompt via omni.REALISM_LOCK. A frame that looks composited is rejected before any clip. Proven: ID_16 S2 overhead test.

## 8. EYE STABILITY and OPENING MOTION (user 2026-11-05, ID 25)
Eye applications (mascara, kajal, liner, shadow): the eye being worked on stays calm and stable: gaze fixed on one point (the camera or a lower mirror), head still, no blinking, twitching or flicker of the lid or lashes during the stroke; the hand moves in one slow steady stroke and the brush or wand reaches the lashes only once. The first frame already has the product at the lashes (stroke in progress). Opening motion (cap, wand, lid): the first frame shows the product already half open (cap off, wand half out); the clip only finishes one short, steady pull over about 2 s; never a long open sequence. Add both sentences to the clip prompt.

## 8b. STROKE-IN-PROGRESS FIRST FRAME (ID 16 apply)
The first frame of an application clip = the open tip touching the skin at the START of the path, NOTHING applied yet: the eye (or lip/cheek) being worked on is completely bare and clean, no line, no smudge, no darkened lash line, no colour ahead of the tip; both eyes otherwise identical and bare. The clip builds the line only along the path of the tip (for an eye: starting at the inner corner of the lower lash line and moving outward). This supersedes any wording that asks for a short trail already laid in the first frame. (Lock 8c below is stricter for eye products: not even a dot.)
## 8c. NOTHING APPLIED IN THE FIRST FRAME, NOT EVEN A DOT (user 2026-11-05, ID 16 Sugar kajal)
Supersedes any wording that allows 'a short trail', 'a tiny dot' or 'a little colour under the tip' in an application first frame for kajal, liner, mascara and other eye products (lip products keep the one-centimetre-trail rule only where the user approved it). For eye products: the skin and lashes are 100 percent bare, the open tip rests on or just above the very start of the line, and there is zero colour anywhere on the skin; the colour appears only in the clip, along the path of the tip. Add to the frame prompt: 'no kajal dot, no mark, no smudge, no darkening anywhere on either eye or lash line'. A frame with any visible mark is rejected before any clip.

## 9. TWO HANDS ONLY (user 2026-11-05)
Exactly one person and exactly two hands (five fingers each) in every frame and clip; never a second set of hands or extra arms (seen in ID 16 apply, redone once). Add 'exactly two hands, one person' to every frame prompt, count the hands on every first frame before any clip (a frame with more than two hands is rejected), and omni.HANDS_LOCK is appended to every clip prompt. Locks 8 (eye stability, opening motion) are also appended automatically (omni.EYE_STABILITY, omni.OPENING_MOTION).

## 10. PRODUCT TEXT LOCK (user 2026-11-05)
Every frame prompt includes the PRODUCT TEXT block from product_text.py (exact front-of-pack words per product). See PIPELINE_CONCEPT_LAB.md 'Product text input'.

## 11. SAME-SURFACE LOCK (user 2026-11-05, ID 52 hook: perfumes on a separate foreground table)
In every creator frame the products sit ON THE SAME SURFACE the creator is using: he or she sits or stands at that table or counter with the forearms or hands resting on it, the products are on it within arm's reach between the creator and the camera, the table edge nearest the camera is the table the creator touches. Never a separate coffee table, tray table or second surface in front of the creator with a visible gap, and never a sofa seat with a table beyond reach. Hook and apply frames: creator seated directly behind the table (table at waist height in the lower third of the frame), products on the table top. Prompt line to add: 'the products are on the same table the person is sitting at and resting their forearms on; no separate table, no gap'. A frame where the products look to be on another surface is rejected at the gate.

## 12. HANDS ENTER FROM BELOW OR THE SIDES, NEVER FROM ABOVE (user 2026-11-05, ID 35 S1_closeup_swiss: both arms hung down from the top edge)
In product close-ups the hands and forearms enter the frame from the BOTTOM edge or the left/right edges (the person is behind or below the camera, arms reaching forward and upward), never from the top edge; no arms hanging down into the frame, no hands appearing above the product with the forearms leaving through the top. The forearms angle upward toward the product from the lower corners, products held at chest height in front of the camera, with the person's torso, if visible, below the hands. Add to every close-up prompt: 'both forearms come up from the bottom edge of the frame; nothing enters from the top edge; natural POV of someone holding the product in front of them'. Reject any close-up where an arm crosses the top edge. omni.HANDS_LOCK adds the same sentence to every clip.

## 13. HOLD LOGIC (user 2026-11-05, ID 35 S4_closeup_forever: a compact stuck on the back of the hand)
Every hold must be physically plausible: the product is supported by a real grip. Allowed holds: pinch grip (thumb and first fingers on the sides or edges), two-hand cup or grip, resting in an open palm held palm-up (fingers slightly curled), cradled in both palms, between fingertips at the edges, laid across the open palm of one hand with the other hand's fingertip steadying it. NEVER: a product balanced or stuck on the back of the hand, on the knuckles or on the wrist, a product held by one fingertip, floating, or leaning on nothing. The back of the hand is only for swatches (a product mark on the skin), never to carry an object. Products keep true size: a compact is about 7-8 cm, a jar about 8 cm, a pencil about 14 cm, a perfume bottle 10-15 cm; a product larger than the hand holding it is rejected. Add to every close-up prompt: 'the product is firmly held by a natural grip (pinch between thumb and fingers at its edges, or resting in an open palm); true size, no balancing, nothing on the back of the hand'.

## 13b. BOTTLE HOLD (amends lock 13; user 2026-11-05: a bottle held like a diya)
No cupped-palms, offering or 'diya' hold for perfume bottles, tubes or any product: both palms held up side by side with the product resting on them reads as offering a lamp. Bottles are held the way people hold bottles: one hand wraps around the body at the middle (fingers around, thumb on the opposite side), cap or nozzle up, label turned to the camera, the other hand either on the base, on the cap or resting on the table; a spray bottle is held by the body with the index finger on the nozzle. Open-palm displays only for flat, light items (a compact or a card) and never for tall bottles. Remove 'cradled in both palms' and 'cupped in both hands' from the allowed holds of lock 13 for bottles and tubes. Add to every bottle prompt: 'held by one hand wrapped around the middle of the bottle like a normal bottle, upright, no cupped palms, no offering pose'.

## 14. HAND SKIN-TONE MATCH (user 2026-11-05)
Hands and forearms in every frame (close-ups included) must match the avatar's own skin tone, undertone and nail style: in hands-only frames the avatar's identity sheet and a face crop are attached as references and the prompt says 'hands and forearms exactly the same skin tone and undertone as the woman/man in the reference, same nail shape and colour'. Each run's 03_character_and_environment_bible.md and concept card carry a `skin_tone` line (e.g. 'warm medium-brown, golden undertone') and `nails` line written from the avatar photo; they are pasted into every frame prompt. At the gate compare the hand tone against the face crop; a visibly lighter, darker or different-undertone hand is rejected. Also keep sleeve and jewellery (bangles, ring) the same as the creator frames.

## 15. THE APPLICATION MUST ACTUALLY HAPPEN (user 2026-11-05, ID 16 kajal)
Lock 8c keeps the first frame bare; the opposite failure is a clip where nothing gets applied. Every application clip prompt states the END STATE explicitly (what the finished mark looks like: where it starts and ends, colour, boldness) and the stroke covers the whole target path, then the finished mark stays visible untouched for the last 1.5 s. omni.APPLY_VISIBLE is appended to every clip prompt. The application framing must be large enough that the mark reads on a phone screen: eye and lash line, or the lips, are at least about 12 percent of the frame height (tighter than the hook). If the finished mark is not clearly visible in the last frame of the delivered clip the clip is flagged as 'not applied' and redone once (with approval).

## 16. NATURALISM: LOOKS LIKE A REAL PHONE VIDEO, NOT A STUDIO (user 2026-11-06: 'too much like AI content, shot in a studio, lacks naturalism')
Every frame and clip reads as a casual phone video a real person filmed at home, not a styled shoot. Image and clip prompts say: 'shot on a phone, handheld or propped on a shelf, ordinary window daylight or a plain room lamp, slightly uneven white balance, mild phone-camera noise and slight softness, no cinematic or studio lighting, no rim light, no glow, no bokeh blur background, no perfect symmetry'. The room is lived-in and slightly untidy and ordinary (a few everyday objects: charger, mug, hairbrush, a folded cloth, mixed clutter), never a curated set (no fairy lights, marigold garlands, rangoli, perfect shelves, candle styling unless the row's festive hook needs ONE small diya at most). The creator wears ordinary everyday home or casual clothes (kurta or t-shirt, no heavy festive styling, no full jewellery sets), natural hair with a few loose strands, visible skin texture (pores, small imperfections, no airbrushed or waxy skin), natural, slightly imperfect expressions (a small real smile, glancing at the product, blinking), unposed hands (natural nails, small creases). Products sit casually (a little angled, not lined up like a catalogue, never a perfectly symmetric row), small natural handheld wobble is allowed in the hook and apply frames only if it stays within lock 4b (the push-in limit is unchanged). omni.NATURALISM is appended to every clip prompt. A frame that looks like a glossy ad or studio shoot is rejected at the gate.

## 17. REALISM RULES FILE (user 2026-11-06)
REALISM_RULES.md (the user's realism note, saved verbatim with a mapping to this pipeline) is part of every run. In particular: ordinary middle-class NEAT homes (not premium, not run-down, no damp or peeling paint, no mess), a different home every video, never a decorated set, festive props minimal, the room is the kind of home only (never a clone of a reference room), a phone held fairly steady with subtle movement and no visible shaking, anatomy count (limbs, hands) checked on every frame at 2 fps or more, no floor or cross-legged poses, product big and exactly its shape, an outfit that fits the campaign (Diwali: a festive but simple outfit), no fire handling or lit diya handling, objects consistent across shots. For a child persona use a milder realism sentence (Omni blocks skin/body wording).

## 18. ARM GEOMETRY AND HANDEDNESS (user 2026-11-06, ID 4 S4_closeup_jhalak)
What went wrong: the prompt said 'hands enter from the bottom or sides' and 'the other hand rests on the table' but not WHICH hand holds the product or WHICH corner each forearm comes from, and a hands-only frame has no torso to anchor the anatomy. The model put both forearms in the same bottom-left corner, crossing, with a second hand lying idle on the table. The old gate (lock 9) only counted hands.
Rules: (1) every close-up prompt carries the clause from `python3 hands_prompt.py <hold> [right|left]` (names the holding hand, the edge each forearm enters from, what the other hand does); (2) a left forearm enters from the bottom-left, a right forearm from the bottom-right, never both from the same corner, never crossing; (3) prefer ONE visible hand: the holding hand in the frame and the other hand out of frame, unless the show device needs two hands (two-hand grip, fan); (4) the thumb and finger layout must match the named hand (check the thumb side: a right hand seen from the back has its thumb on the left); (5) gate before any clip: trace each forearm from the wrist to a frame edge: one continuous forearm per hand, two forearms at most, entering from different sides, nothing crossing, bangles or sleeves identical on both wrists; a frame that fails is redone once (with approval) BEFORE any clip is generated; (6) frames are approved before clip generation starts (clips are never generated from an unchecked frame).

## 19. UPPER-MIDDLE-CLASS HOME BACKGROUND (user 2026-11-06; supersedes the 'ordinary middle-class, not premium' wording of lock 17 and REALISM_RULES.md)
Every background is a real room of an UPPER-MIDDLE-CLASS Indian apartment: tasteful and neat but lived in, not luxury and not a showroom. Typical: wooden or modular furniture, a false ceiling with recessed lights, a modular wardrobe, a wall TV with a console, a fabric sofa, a dining table with a crockery cabinet, tiled or light-wood floors, curtains or blinds, a ceiling fan, a few family photos and a plant; a couple of ordinary objects on the table edge (remote, charger, hairbrush, jug); no fairy lights, no flowers, no decor props, never a kitchen. Rooms: living room (centre table), bedroom dressing table, dining room (dining table), study desk. A different room every video, locked with one empty-room plate; the creator sits at the table or desk shown. The four approved test rooms are in room_tests_upper_middle/ (living room, bedroom dressing table, dining room, study desk); use them as the style class, not as a room to clone. Prompt line: 'a room of an upper-middle-class Indian apartment, neat and tasteful but lived in, not luxury, not a showroom'.
